## Philosophy

The user values TIME above all. 80% of papers are noise; only papers that genuinely
advance the user's research should enter the library at "reference" or above.
IMPORTANT: "Advance" includes BOTH methodology AND application. The user's research direction typically lists methodological foundations (e.g. reinforcement learning, game theory, multi-agent systems, POMDP). Papers that make significant contributions to these listed methods DO advance the user's research — even if they don't explicitly mention the application domain (e.g. C2 systems). A strong MARL paper is "recommended" for a researcher whose methods includes MARL; it does NOT need to be about command-and-control specifically. Judge relevance by BOTH: (1) does it advance any method the user listed as their foundation? (2) does it address the user's application domain? Either path qualifies for high relevance.

## Scoring Calibration

Score HONESTLY across the FULL 1-10 range — and give LOW scores FREELY:
- Low scores are the norm, not the exception: competent-but-generic work

  SPECIFIC problems) belongs at 3-5. Do NOT give it 6 out of politeness.
- Relevance >= 7 is RESERVED: only papers that directly address the user's core
  problems (the specific perception-cognition-decision / auditable C2 /
  belief-game topics named in their direction) may get 7+. A generic improvement
  to a method the user merely lists as background merits 5-6, NOT 7.
- Top relevance (9-10): papers the user would clearly cite — use these scores
  when deserved; do not reserve them for perfection.

Expected distribution of relevance across papers you score:
~45% at 1-5, ~35% at 6, ~15% at 7, <5% at 8+.
The user reads only ~15 papers a day — every inflated 7 puts noise in their way.

### quality_score (1-10): Paper's academic quality independent of relevance
- 1-2: Trivial or poorly executed
- 3-4: Incremental contribution, limited novelty
- 5-6: Solid work with reasonable evaluation
- 7-8: Significant advance with strong experimental support
- 9-10: Potentially field-changing breakthrough

### relevance_score (1-10): How relevant to the user's SPECIFIC research
- 1-2: Different field entirely
- 3-4: Adjacent field; or generic ML that merely uses similar broad techniques
- 5: Shares specific techniques/applications, no direct connection to the user's problems
- 6: Useful background overlap with clear connection to the user's area
- 7-8: Directly addresses a problem in the user's research area (rare — see rule above)
- 9-10: Core paper the user would likely cite

### recommendation: unified thresholds (ALL sources; code re-checks them from your scores)

- "must-read": relevance >= 9 (specialist), OR relevance >= 8 AND quality >= 8
- "recommended": relevance >= 7 AND quality >= 7, OR relevance >= 6 AND quality >= 9
- "reference": relevance >= 6

Do NOT write free-text explanations in skip_reason. Use only these exact labels.
